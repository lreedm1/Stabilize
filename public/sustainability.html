Sustainable growth

A useful tool also has to survive.

Stabilize should not grow faster than its safety, evidence, and economics. The goal is not maximum traffic. The goal is a dependable public tool whose operating model can support model usage, maintenance, review, and improvement without exploiting the people who need it.

The business thesis

Keep a constrained and useful core accessible. Charge for added capability and convenience that does not determine whether someone can get basic help. Let organizations pay for bounded pilots, implementation, training, evaluation, and support. Use grants and sponsorships for public access, independent review, and research that the market may not fund on its own.

This is a cross-subsidy model: paid value supports public value. It is more durable than expecting the person in the most depleted state to carry the full cost of the system.

What should remain accessible

  • A short conversation focused on reducing load and finding one next step.
  • Deterministic routing for certain urgent safety, medical, shelter, and medication situations.
  • A usable guest experience beginning on GPT-5.6 Fast without requiring an account.
  • Fifty GPT-5.6 Fast messages per UTC day for signed-in free accounts before GPT-5.4 fallback.
  • Public explanations of the floor-first approach, limits, privacy behavior, and responsible use.

Free use still needs rate limits, abuse controls, and a fixed monthly infrastructure budget. “Free” should describe access for the person, not pretend that operating the service has no cost.

Who can pay

Individuals

Signed-in free accounts receive 50 GPT-5.6 Fast messages per UTC day before GPT-5.4 fallback. The current optional subscription enables choice between GPT-5.4 and Current and provides 200 non-default-model messages per UTC month. Future paid features should be added only when user research shows they solve a real problem, such as useful continuity, saved plans, higher limits, or better follow-through.

Boundary: no safety-critical capability should depend on payment.

Organizations

Universities, community programs, housing providers, public agencies, and health organizations can fund fixed-scope pilots. They would pay for implementation, configured resource routing, staff training, evaluation, uptime expectations, and support—not access to individual chats.

Boundary: no employee, school, insurer, or partner surveillance.

Grants and sponsors

Philanthropic and public-interest funding can underwrite free usage, accessibility, multilingual work, safety review, and evaluation before those activities have a direct buyer.

Boundary: funding does not buy influence over individual responses.

A 12-month validation sequence

  1. Months 0–3: measure the real system. Track cost per completed session, response latency, user-reported usefulness, voluntary return use, failure modes, and safety handoffs without storing prompt bodies in analytics. Add hard usage budgets and independent safety, privacy, and security review.
  2. Months 3–6: test one paid individual offer. Make the offer visible, keep the promise narrow, interview paying and nonpaying users, and judge it by activation, retention, support burden, and gross margin rather than sign-up count alone.
  3. Months 6–9: run two or three paid organizational pilots. Use fixed scope, explicit privacy terms, a named operational owner, baseline measures, and a written evaluation. Do not build custom features without a repeatable use case.
  4. Months 9–12: choose deliberately. Expand only if the product is useful, incidents are manageable, paid use subsidizes public use, and at least one organization wants to renew. Otherwise narrow the product, redesign the offer, pause growth, or stop the failed approach.

What success should mean

Stabilize should not count “lives saved,” because the product cannot establish that claim. A more honest scorecard combines user value, safety, reliability, and economics.

  • People report greater clarity or a workable next step.
  • A meaningful share act on the step or return voluntarily.
  • Known failure modes are detected, reviewed, and reduced.
  • Paid use has enough margin to subsidize public usage and continued review.
  • Organizational pilots renew because they create measurable value without compromising privacy.
  • The project maintains a cash reserve instead of operating one invoice away from shutdown.

Financial guardrails

  • Set a fixed monthly ceiling for free inference and increase it only from recurring revenue or restricted grant funding.
  • Do not buy paid traffic until organic users understand the product and a meaningful share return.
  • Do not hire permanent staff before the project has repeatable revenue and at least six months of runway.
  • Price pilots to include founder time, support, evaluation, and risk—not only cloud costs.
  • Reserve money for independent safety, privacy, security, and legal review before feature expansion.

Nonnegotiable boundaries

Stabilize should not fund itself with advertising, sale of conversation data, manipulative engagement, paywalled emergency routing, or organizational access to individual chats. A revenue model that undermines trust would defeat the product’s purpose.
