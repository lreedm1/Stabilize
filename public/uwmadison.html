When everything feels like too much, find one practical next step.

Stabilize is a free, non-clinical AI support tool for overloaded moments. It helps you notice what may be blocking the next step, choose one manageable action, and connect with a person or campus resource when that would help.

What the tool is for

Start with what is blocking the next step.

Overload is not always one problem. A student may be trying to solve school, work, housing, money, health, or a relationship while also being hungry, exhausted, isolated, overstimulated, or unable to organize the logistics. Stabilize is designed to reduce that load without taking control.

1

Notice the immediate need

Safety, food or water, rest, prescribed-care logistics, shelter, connection, sensory calm, transportation, or another urgent practical barrier.

2

Choose one manageable action

Prefer a small, reversible step that can produce useful evidence over a dramatic decision made under pressure.

3

Move toward real support

The useful outcome may be food, sleep, a phone call, a campus appointment, a safe person, or qualified professional care—not a longer chat.

Scope: Stabilize is not therapy, diagnosis, medical advice, emergency care, or a substitute for a professional who can assess and respond in the real world. AI responses can be incomplete or wrong.

Why a campus-specific doorway may help

Basic-needs strain is already part of student life.

27%

of UW–Madison undergraduates in the university's spring 2025 Basic Needs Survey reported food insecurity during the prior 30 days.

More than 1 in 3

surveyed students reported housing insecurity during the prior 12 months.

The goal is not to duplicate UW services. It is to make the first move easier when a student is overloaded, then point toward the university and community systems that can actually provide food, housing, care, advocacy, or other support.

A responsible path onto campus

Review first. Test small. Expand only with evidence.

This page proposes a bounded campus pilot—not a university endorsement, clinical deployment, or blanket promotion.

  1. 1. Campus resource and safety review

    Ask Basic Needs and OSAS to check the UW links, language, escalation boundaries, and missing referral routes. The initial request is for review, not adoption.

  2. 2. Student co-design

    Invite mental-health and basic-needs student organizations to test wording, accessibility, usefulness, and difficult scenarios. Students should be designers, not merely a distribution channel.

  3. 3. Four-week closed pilot

    Begin with roughly 10–25 voluntary participants. Keep recruitment limited, provide clear limits, and make leaving for human support easy.

  4. 4. One-page results report

    Report whether participants finished, knew their next step, selected a campus resource, chose human support, or reported a helpful or harmful experience. Record safety failures and corrections.

  5. 5. Decide whether broader distribution is warranted

    Expand only if students understand the tool, the referral map is accurate, serious failures are addressed, and campus reviewers believe a larger test is responsible.

Campus pilot boundaries

  • No advertising or marketing trackers on this UW landing page.
  • No paid-tier promotion on the pilot page.
  • No sale of student conversation data.
  • No institution access to individual conversations.
  • Minimal aggregate measures tied to a specific decision.

Stop conditions

  • A serious safety or privacy failure pauses distribution.
  • Incorrect campus referrals are corrected before continued outreach.
  • Evidence of confusion about university endorsement triggers redesign.
  • Low usefulness leads to a smaller scope or a stopped pilot—not more promotion.

Measurement principle: Do not optimize for time in chat. A useful exit into food, rest, a safe person, a campus resource, or professional care is a stronger outcome than a long conversation.

Privacy, accessibility, and trust

Make the boundary visible before asking for trust.

This UW landing page contains no advertising scripts, marketing trackers, chat history, or sign-in. Cloudflare still processes ordinary request metadata to deliver and secure the page. Selecting Use Stabilize opens the main Stabilize service, and the main service's privacy and provider-processing terms then apply.
