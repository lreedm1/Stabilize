Why this exists

Built for the next person

I am a suicide survivor. I built Stabilize for the metaphorical next person: someone who, like me, struggled to find meaning in continuing to live.

The beginning

The first idea was not to build a machine that could argue someone into living. It was to create something I wish had been available when pain, exhaustion, and isolation narrowed what I could see: a tool that could reduce the immediate load, notice what had been neglected, and help identify one next step that was still possible.

What Stabilize became

Over time, the project evolved into a stabilizing protocol focused on reducing the risk of foreseeable tragedy. Many terrible outcomes are not caused by one dramatic failure. They grow through ordinary needs going unattended: safety, food, water, sleep, prescribed care, shelter, connection, sensory calm, or an urgent practical problem that no one helped make manageable.

The protocol begins with a simple premise: a bad state is not a bad life, and a depleted body is a poor judge. Stabilize tries to answer the person’s actual request while noticing whether an immediate need changes what should happen next.

Why artificial intelligence

AI cannot replace friendship, community, clinicians, emergency services, or a person’s own judgment. But it can be available in a quiet moment, help separate facts from the story forming around them, and bring attention back to a neglected need before that need becomes a larger crisis.

The aim is not to take control. It is to reduce cognitive load, preserve agency, and offer a small, reversible action that remains possible even when capacity is low.

The goal

My goal with Stabilize is to help the world become a more stable place through artificial intelligence that brings attention to our neglected needs. I want the tool to help people avoid preventable harm, protect what matters before it is lost, and find enough stability to keep choosing what comes next.

Stabilize is still an evolving public prototype. Its purpose remains the same: build something useful for the next person.

Limits: Stabilize is not emergency care, therapy, diagnosis, or a guarantee of safety. In immediate danger or a medical emergency, contact a person or service able to respond now.
