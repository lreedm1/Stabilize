About Stabilize

Built for the next person—and built to last.

I am a suicide survivor. I started Stabilize because I wanted the next person in an overloaded, narrowed state to have help finding one manageable next step. That experience explains the mission. It does not, by itself, prove that the product works.

Why it exists

The first idea was not to build a machine that could argue someone into living. It was to create something I wish had been available when pain, exhaustion, and isolation narrowed what I could see: a tool that could reduce the immediate load, notice a neglected need, and help identify one action that was still possible.

What Stabilize is trying to do

Stabilize is a floor-first AI support tool for overloaded moments. It tries to answer the person’s actual request while noticing whether safety, food, water, sleep, prescribed care, shelter, connection, sensory calm, or an urgent practical problem changes what should happen next.

Its working premise is modest: a bad state is not necessarily a bad life, and a depleted body is a poor judge. The intended outcome is not a dramatic transformation. It is less confusion, a protected basic need, or one safe and reversible next step.

What it cannot claim

Stabilize is an early public prototype. It has not been clinically validated, it cannot predict a crisis, and it should not claim that it prevents suicide or other serious harm. It is not therapy, diagnosis, emergency care, or a substitute for people and services able to respond in the real world.

The project has to earn trust through testing, independent review, careful measurement, reliable operation, and clear limits—not through the strength of its origin story.

Why artificial intelligence

AI can be available at a quiet or inconvenient moment, help separate facts from the story forming around them, and reduce the work required to choose a next step. It can also be wrong, overly confident, inconsistent, or unsafe. That is why Stabilize combines model responses with fixed routing for certain urgent situations and keeps its claims narrow.

The aim is not to take control. It is to reduce cognitive load, preserve agency, and make a useful action easier to begin.

Impact requires sustainability

A tool cannot have durable impact if it depends indefinitely on free infrastructure, unpaid founder labor, or occasional grants. Model usage, hosting, monitoring, accessibility work, security, evaluation, support, and independent safety review all cost money. A product that cannot pay for those functions remains fragile no matter how worthy its mission is.

The intended business model is a cross-subsidy: keep a constrained and useful public core accessible, charge for added convenience and capacity, sell clearly bounded services to organizations, and use grants or sponsorships to fund public access and evidence-building.

  • Public core: short stabilization support, deterministic urgent routing, guest access beginning on GPT-5.6 Fast, plus 50 GPT-5.6 Fast messages per UTC day for signed-in accounts before GPT-5.4 fallback.
  • Optional individual plan: subscriber choice between GPT-5.4 and Current plus 200 non-default-model messages per UTC month, and only later any continuity features that prove useful and are not safety-critical.
  • Organizational pilots: paid setup, training, evaluation, and support without giving an organization access to individual conversations.
  • Grants and sponsorships: funding for free usage, accessibility, independent review, and public-interest evaluation.

Revenue boundary: emergency routing and the basic ability to ask for help should not become premium features. Paid offerings should fund reliability and added value, not exploit fear, urgency, or sensitive conversation data.

What must be proven

Stabilize should not scale from attention alone. Before expanding, it needs evidence that people understand the product, find the response useful, can act on the next step, return voluntarily, and do not experience unacceptable harm. It also needs to know the real cost of a useful session and whether individuals or organizations will pay enough to support the free core.

  • User-reported clarity, usefulness, and action taken.
  • Safety incidents, failure modes, and successful human handoffs.
  • Retention without manipulative engagement tactics.
  • Cost per useful session and gross margin on paid use.
  • Real willingness to pay, renew, and recommend—not just verbal enthusiasm.

Current status

Stabilize is a founder-led public prototype with real operating costs and limited evidence. Guest ordinary chats begin on GPT-5.6 Fast. Signed-in free accounts receive 50 GPT-5.6 Fast messages per UTC day before GPT-5.4 fallback. The current paid model-allowance subscription enables subscriber model choice and 200 non-default-model messages per UTC month. It is an early payment experiment, not a complete business model. The project will grow only if it can show both usefulness and a credible way to finance reliable operation over time.

Limits: Stabilize is not emergency care, therapy, diagnosis, or a guarantee of safety. In immediate danger or a medical emergency, contact a person or service able to respond now.
